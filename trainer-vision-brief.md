# Trainer's Vision Brief
*(Upload to project knowledge. This is the vision as the training should reflect it, implicitly. The full vision and strategy live in the separate AI Vision project. Update this brief only when the vision changes meaningfully.)*

## What the training should leave people believing
1. **AI is understandable.** It isn't magic or a threat out of science fiction. Once you see how it works, the fear goes down.
2. **AI is useful today, within limits.** It can measurably speed up real fab work. It is not "everything it's cracked up to be," and it is not what the fearmongers say either.
3. **You stay in charge.** AI makes skilled people faster. It does not replace their judgment or expertise.
4. **AI is an engine; the value is in the engineering around it.** The future of AI at work is mostly AI built into tools with harnesses and safety constraints, not people typing into chatbots.
5. **This company is serious and competent about AI.** The training itself shows that AI is being adopted thoughtfully, by people who understand it.

## How it shows up
- Never stated as a "vision" section or slide.
- It comes through in the **tone**: honest, evidence-based, no hype, focused on value add rather than preaching.
- Fear and myth, especially "AI will take everyone's jobs," are answered indirectly: people learn the tool, see its shortcomings, and stay in charge of the work. The trainer does not promise anything about jobs and does not present research on the subject: this is a training, not a lecture.
- The engine analogy (point 4) is used only where it helps explain something practical, never as a lesson of its own.
- Every course also **sells**: attendees should leave wanting to use AI, and able to picture adapting it to their own fab.
