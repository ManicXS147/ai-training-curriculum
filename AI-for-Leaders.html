AI for Leaders

Sharpen a decision.

Presented by Andrew Hughes

AI Basics taught everyone, leaders included, how these tools work and how to talk to them. This session is about what changes when you lead. We'll work one practical case, start to finish, and stop at every step to ask why it's there. The case is the vehicle. The principles are what you take home, along with the playbook. 50 minutes, three short exercises, one question break, an open forum.

Blast radius: how far a decision travels. A leader's travels furthest, so the quality bar is higher. AI is very good at insight: reading everything, tracing claims, listing what's assumed, arguing the other side. It shouldn't make the call. The judgment stays with you. (Space.) If you took Basics, you've seen the car: the model is the engine, and you're the driver. Today is about driving well.

One workflow, seven steps: Sharpen a decision. The outlined steps are yours; the rest are AI's. Notice the AI never gets the decide step. (Space.) Every step teaches one of three things: when you step in, how to review AI work using AI, and how to own what goes out. Every step also has a principle that works on any decision, not just today's. You take the whole playbook home.
01

Frame it

The case: move two etch tool sets onto one line by Q3 to save $1.2M a year. You decide at Friday's staff meeting.

The plan is in the kit: consolidation_plan.txt. One page, drafted with AI for the operations staff meeting. It's invented, and it has some problems we'll find together. Before anyone opens a chat, step 1 is yours: framing the decision.

Step 1 · You

Frame it before AI sees anything.

Decision
The question, in one sentence
Options
Each one, including doing nothing
Deadline
When, and why then
Blast radius
Who and what it lands on
Undo cost
What reversing it would cost
Criteria
What a good outcome looks like
Leaning
What you favor now, and why

PrincipleYou own the question. Say what you need before AI sees anything.

Seven things every decision has. In the playbook, the frame prompt interviews you: you talk freely, it rates each one Clear, Fuzzy or Unknown, asks up to three questions a round, and turns anything still open into an open item. You confirm the result. Why first: AI can only analyze the decision you hand it. If you hand it the plan, it analyzes the plan's question. Your frame makes it analyze yours. (Space.) The principle, for any decision: you own the question. Lesson 1, when you step in: at the start.

Step 1 · You

Write your leaning down. Then leave it out.

decision_frame.txt

Goes to the chat that builds the brief

my_leaning.txt

Goes only to the chat that argues with you

Telling AI to ignore something doesn't stop it steering. Remove it; don't forbid it.

PrincipleKeep your own preference out of the analysis.

The frame prompt puts your leaning in its own block for a reason. If the brief chat knows you want to approve, the brief drifts toward approval. You won't see it happen. (Space.) So you don't tell it "ignore my leaning." You don't give it your leaning at all. In the kit, that's two files. Only one of them goes to the brief. (Space.) Principle: keep your preference out of the analysis. Your leaning comes back in step 4, where it gets tested.

02

Build and review

AI builds the insight. A second chat checks it.

Steps 2 and 3. Both AI's, both checked against something written down.

Step 2 · AI · The Decision Brief standard

Ask for insight against a standard, not for an answer.

Claims
Every claim, quoted, with its evidence or "no evidence"
Figures
Every figure recomputed
Assumptions
Every one rated for impact and certainty
Options
Each one compared on every criterion
Gaps
What's missing that could change the decision
Never
A recommendation. The judgment stays with you.

PrincipleA written standard turns "looks right" into rules you can check.

The build prompt translates the plan into a Decision Brief that meets this standard. Each item gets an ID, so later steps can point at it. (Space.) And one thing it never contains: a recommendation. That's "insight, not decision," written into the standard. The judgment stays with you. (Space.) Principle: a standard is a checklist that makes sure the right things are there. "Looks good to me" isn't checkable; "every figure recomputed" is. Lesson 2.

Step 3 · AI, in a fresh chat

Work and review never share a chat.

Chat B · buildThe brief
Chat C · reviewPlan + brief + standard + gate
Back to chat BPatch only what failed

The gate: patch anything wrong or missing, and anything misleading that two readers could read differently.

PrincipleCheck the output against the source and the standard, in a fresh chat.

The chat that wrote the brief is the worst place to check it: it already believes its own work. A new chat sees only the plan, the brief, the standard and the gate. (Space.) The gate scores each finding: Impact 1 to 5, Ambiguity 0 to 2, with written anchors. Patch when Impact is 4 or 5, or 3 with Ambiguity 2. Mechanical failures, like a claim with no quote, are patched without scoring. The review lists failures only. (Space.) Principle, for any AI output that matters: review it fresh, against the source and a standard. This is lesson 2 in full: reviewing AI work, using AI.

03

Challenge and decide

With a high blast radius, hearing only what you want is expensive.

Steps 4 and 5. Your leaning comes back in, so it can be tested. Then you decide.

Step 4 · AI, in a fresh chat

Use AI to argue with you, not agree with you.

In

The fixed brief, and your leaning

Out

The case against, cited to brief IDs · a pre-mortem · what evidence would change your mind

PrincipleAsk for the strongest case against what you already believe.

A chatbot follows the framing of the question. Ask "is this a good plan?" and you'll usually hear yes. So you hand it your leaning and ask for the strongest case against it, built only from the brief, with every point pointing at a brief ID. (Space.) Principle: ask for the case against. It sharpens your judgment; it doesn't make the decision. Lessons 1 and 2.

04

Own it

You decided. Now you own what goes out.

Steps 6 and 7: record the decision, announce it, and improve the playbook.

Step 6 · You

Write the decision in your own words.

Decision
Approve with changes: the shutdown moves after the customer audit
Why
No slack before the audit (AS-3); savings unsourced (CL-2)
Would change it
Finance sources the $1.2M; customers confirm no requalification
Owners
Finance: savings basis · Customer quality: requalification
The Decision Record is a template, not a prompt. You write it, because it's your decision. Note the "Why" points at brief IDs, so anyone can trace it back. The full template also has who it lands on and what's still open. This is the example decision for the case. Yours might differ, and that's fine.

PrincipleYou own what goes out. AI helps word your decision; it doesn't make it.

The announcement prompt takes only your Decision Record and the audience. It adds no content of its own, keeps your words for the decision itself, and always says how to raise a concern. (Space.) A decision people can challenge is one they'll trust. (Space.) Principle: you own what goes out under your name. Lesson 3.

05

Lead your team

Your team copies what you do, not what the policy says.

Transition, out loud: "That's your own decision. Now your team."

Your team's habits

Ask "How was it checked?"
not "Did you use AI?"

Hidden AI use is worse than open use. Make telling you the easy path.

If "did you use AI?" sounds like an accusation, people stop telling you. Then you're judging work without knowing how it was made. (Space.) Ask how it was checked instead. It rewards the review step you just practised, and it works whether AI was used or not.

The question you'll get

"Is this replacing me?"

Answer from your own lane: what you'll use it for on this team, and what stays with people.

You will get this, usually privately. (Space.) Don't promise what isn't yours to promise. Do say what's true on your team: the framing, the checks, the decision and the sign-off stay with people. You just watched that in seven steps.
