AI for Leaders · Your playbook · v1.0

Sharpen a decision

Your decisions land on more people than anyone else's. Use AI for insight, not decisions: it finds the evidence, the gaps and the case against; you frame, decide and sign. Copy this playbook into your own document and improve it every time it misses.

The workflow

SDecision Brief standard

  1. [Lint] Every claim the decision rests on is listed, with its quote.
  2. [Review] Every claim shows its evidence and where it is, or NO EVIDENCE.
  3. [Review] Every figure is recomputed; a mismatch shows both values.
  4. [Review] Every assumption is tied to a quote or NOT IN SOURCE, rated for impact and certainty.
  5. [Lint] Every option is compared on every criterion.
  6. [Review] Missing information that could change the decision is an open item.
  7. [Lint] The brief contains no recommendation.

Lint rules are mechanical and always patched. Review rules need judgment and go through the gate.

GReview Gate

Impact

1 Cosmetic · 2 Local · 3 Misleading (unclear, and someone could act on it) · 4 Wrong (differs from the source, or conditional shown as final) · 5 Missing or invented

Ambiguity

0 Two readers read it the same · 1 Small differences · 2 Could read it differently, or can't be checked

Gate

Patch when Impact is 4 or 5, or Impact 3 with Ambiguity 2. Keep in every other case.

1Frame the decision · chat A

Condition: you face a decision that lands on others.

<decision_talk> [Talk freely: the decision, why now, the options you see, who it affects, and what you lean toward] </decision_talk> <inputs> - decision_talk and my answers: free talk. Sort every specific I mention as BINDING, EXAMPLE, BACKGROUND or GUESS, and restate examples generally. </inputs> <goal> Interview me, then translate my answers into a Decision Frame that a separate chat can analyze without talking to me. Success: every dimension is Clear or carried as an open item, and I confirm the frame. </goal> <dimensions> 1. Decision: the question, in one sentence. 2. Options: each option, including doing nothing. 3. Deadline: when it must be decided, and why. 4. Blast radius: who and what it lands on. 5. Reversibility: what undoing it would cost. 6. Criteria: what a good outcome looks like, and how it would be measured. 7. 2Build the brief · chat B (new)

Condition: you have a confirmed frame. Leave your leaning out.

<source_material> [Upload or paste the plan, proposal or work the decision rests on] </source_material> <decision_frame> [Upload or paste the confirmed Decision Frame, dimensions 1 to 6] </decision_frame> <inputs> - source_material: the source for every claim, figure and assumption. - decision_frame: sets the question, options and criteria; adds no facts. </inputs> <goal> Translate the source material into a Decision Brief that meets the Decision Brief Standard, so I can decide with the evidence, assumptions and gaps in view. Success: every item passes DB1 to DB7. </goal> <standard> DB1 [Lint] Every claim the decision rests on is listed, with its quote. DB2 [Review] Every claim shows its evidence and where it is, or NO EVIDENCE. DB3 [Review] Every figure is recomputed, and a mismatch shows both values. DB4 [Review] Every assumption is tied to a quote or marked NOT IN SOURCE, and is rated for impact and certainty with the anchors. DB5 [Lint] Every option in the frame is compared on every criterion in the frame. DB6 [Review] Information that is absent and could change the decision is an open item. DB7 [Lint] The brief contains no recommendation. </standard> <limits> - Labels: NO EVIDENCE, FIGURE FAILS, NOT IN SOURCE, SOURCE TO OPEN. - Impact: H = the decision's goal fails if this is wrong; M = cost or schedule slips; L = minor rework. - Certainty: H = the source gives evidence; M = stated without evidence; L = not stated or contradicted. </limits> <output> 1. Assumptions, high impact and low certainty first: | ID (AS-n) | Assumption | Quote or NOT IN SOURCE | Impact | Certainty | 4. Options: | Option | one column per criterion | AS IDs it depends on | 5. Open items: | ID (OPEN-n) | Missing information | How it could change the decision | 6. Sources to open: | Source | Why | </output>

Condition: the brief is built. Never in the chat that built it.

<source> [Upload or paste what the brief was built from] </source> <artifact> [Paste the Decision Brief] </artifact> <standard> DB1 [Lint] Every claim the decision rests on is listed, with its quote. DB4 [Review] Every assumption is tied to a quote or marked NOT IN SOURCE, and is rated for impact and certainty with the anchors. DB5 [Lint] Every option in the frame is compared on every criterion in the frame. DB6 [Review] Information that is absent and could change the decision is an open item. DB7 [Lint] The brief contains no recommendation. </standard> <gate> Impact (I): 1 Cosmetic: wording only; meaning unchanged. 2 Local: one item is weaker than it should be; nothing depends on it. 3 Misleading: an owner, date, figure or status is unclear, and someone could act on it. 4 Wrong: an owner, date, figure or decision differs from the source, or a conditional item is shown as final. 5 Missing or invented: something in the source has no item, or an item has no source. PPrompt Grader · before you share a prompt

Condition: before a prompt goes to your team, or after you change one. New chat. Rerun the revised prompt on the same input before you save it.

<rubric> Score each criterion 1 to 3. A criterion with nothing to act on scores 3 if the prompt states that case and how to handle it. | Criterion | 1 — Legacy | 2 — Developing | 3 — Standard | | --- | --- | --- | --- | | 1. Context | Vague, or includes material the task doesn't use. | Right material, but not delimited, or long material follows the goal with no restatement. | Only material the task uses, each piece labeled and delimited. Long material first; goal and output after. | | 2. Goal | Scripts the model's reasoning ("think step by step"). | Goal mixed with procedure the task doesn't need. | States the goal and success criteria. Steps only where their order is part of the result or a person takes part. | | 3. Limits | Vague bans ("don't be wordy") or no limits. | Basic limits; edge cases open. | Positive, specific limits. A named label or action for each kind of uncertainty. Claims cite sources when sources exist. | | 4. Output | Open, or described casually. | General form only ("use a table"). | Parts in order, each part's form and limits, any labels or IDs. Working before the final block. JSON only for programs. | | 5. Signal | Filler, personas, threats, tips. | Some padding. | Every sentence serves context, goal, limits or output. Definitions needed to stand alone stay. | | 6. Inputs and handoffs | Demands a specification from the person, or takes a raw chat or summary. | Treats every detail from a person as binding, or the next step can't check the output. | States how each input is treated. A person's free talk fills a stated rubric: named dimensions, each rated Clear, Fuzzy or Unknown against written definitions, with an exit rule; anything unresolved becomes an open item. Specifics are sorted as binding, example, background or guess; examples restated generally. The handoff translates what the person wants into a finite, testable artifact built to a named standard for its type (requirements: INCOSE rules, EARS syntax); it is a translation, never a summary; it lists open items and is confirmed by the person. A rubric encoding that standard checks it: a lint for mechanical rules, an anchored review for judgment. The next prompt takes only the artifact, in a new thread. | Rules: - Name no particular system, product, site, vendor or person; specifics arrive as inputs. - Remove unwanted details from the input instead of telling the model to ignore them. - A ban naming one exact item ("never write TBD") is allowed. - Show formats with empty templates, not filled examples. - State artifacts directly, without process references ("from Stage 4"). - Review in a new thread, with the output, the artifact it came from, and the person's words only. - Fix a thread's own artifact in that thread; hand on only the fixed artifact. - Follow the Agile cycle: build from the artifact, the person reviews the result, new or changed requirements go into the artifact as a versioned patch with a change-log line, and the next cycle starts a new thread from the revised artifact. - Before saving a changed prompt, rerun it on the same input. </rubric> <prompt_under_review> [PASTE THE PROMPT TO GRADE] </prompt_under_review> <purpose> [One line: what the prompt is for and who runs it. Write NOT STATED if unknown.] </purpose> <goal> Grade the prompt under review against every rubric criterion and rule, and write a revised prompt that does the same job and scores 3 on all six criteria. Success: every score below 3 has evidence and a fix, and the revised prompt changes only what the fixes require. </goal> <limits> - Use the rubric cell wording as the anchor for each score. - Evidence is the exact quoted line, or MISSING plus what is missing. - When the purpose is NOT STATED and a score depends on it, write ASSUMED and the purpose you assumed. - Keep the prompt's task, inputs and intended output. Record each change in the change list, outside the revised prompt. - Revised prompt length: the original length plus only what the fixes add. </limits> <output> 1. Scorecard: table with columns Criterion | Score (1-3) | Evidence | Fix; rows 1 to 6, then one row per rule broken. 2. Change list: numbered, one line per change, each naming its criterion or rule. 3. Revised prompt: one fenced code block, ready to paste. 4. Open items: decisions the person must make before saving the revised prompt, or NONE. 5. Retest: one line naming the input to rerun the revised prompt on before saving. </output>
