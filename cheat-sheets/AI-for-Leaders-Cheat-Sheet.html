AI for Leaders · Your playbook · v1.1

Sharpen a decision

Your decisions land on more people than anyone else's. Use AI for insight, not decisions: it finds the evidence, the gaps and the case against; you frame, decide and sign. Works in Copilot Chat and Gemini, which have no content restrictions. The shared copy lives in the AI training SharePoint folder: copy it into your own document and improve it every time it misses.

S

Decision Brief standard

  1. [Lint] Every claim, figure and assumption row has an ID and a quote or NOT IN SOURCE.
  2. [Review] Every claim the decision rests on is listed, with its evidence and where, or NO EVIDENCE.
  3. [Review] Every figure is recomputed from the source; a mismatch shows both values.
  4. [Review] Every assumption, stated or implied, is rated for impact and certainty.
  5. [Lint] Every option has a cell for every criterion.
  6. [Review] Missing information that could change the decision is an open item, including what the frame needs and the source lacks.
  7. [Lint] The brief contains no recommendation and no ranking of options.

Lint rules are mechanical and always patched. Review rules need judgment and go through the gate.

G

Review Gate

Impact

1 Cosmetic · 2 Local · 3 Misleading (unclear, and someone could act on it) · 4 Wrong (differs from the source, or an assumption shown as a fact) · 5 Missing or invented

Ambiguity

0 Two readers read it the same · 1 Small differences · 2 Could read it differently, or can't be checked

Gate

Patch when Impact is 4 or 5, or Impact 3 with Ambiguity 2. Keep in every other case.

Frame the decision

chat A

Condition: you face a decision that lands on others.

<decision_talk> [Talk freely: the decision, why now, the options you see, who it affects, and what you lean toward] </decision_talk> <inputs> - decision_talk and my answers: free talk. Sort every specific I mention (a name, number, system, date or story) as BINDING, EXAMPLE, BACKGROUND or GUESS. BINDING: the decision must respect exactly this. EXAMPLE: one instance of a general need; restate it generally. BACKGROUND: true, but constrains nothing. GUESS: my estimate; it becomes an open item. </inputs> <goal> Interview me, then translate my answers into Decision Frame v1.0, built to the frame standard, that a separate chat can analyze without talking to me. Success: every dimension is Clear or carried as an open item, every frame line meets the standard, and I confirm the frame. </goal> <dimensions> 1. Decision: the question, in one sentence. 2. Options: each option, including doing nothing. 3. Deadline: when it must be decided, and why. 4. Blast radius: every group, customer, commitment and function it lands on. 5. Undo cost: what undoing it would cost, and when undoing gets harder. 6. Criteria: what a good outcome looks like, and how it would be measured. 7. Leaning: which option I favor now, and why. </dimensions> <status_definitions> Clear: an explicit answer with one reasonable reading. Fuzzy: answered, but vague, conflicting, or open to two readings. Unknown: not yet discussed, or I said I don't know. </status_definitions> <frame_standard> F1 One fact per line. F2 Every line quotes or cites my words; a line you inferred is an open item instead. F3 Every criterion names how it would be judged met or not met. F4 Options are distinct, and one of them is doing nothing or keeping things as they are. F5 Every GUESS and every unresolved point is an OPEN-n item naming what would close it. Never write TBD. F6 The leaning appears only in its own block. </frame_standard> <limits> - Each round: the status of dimensions 1 to 7, the new specifics with a proposed sort, then up to 3 questions, each with options A, B, C and D ("None of these, or I don't know"). Options are distinct answers of comparable appeal. - A specific stays unsorted until I confirm its sort. - Exit: when all seven are Clear, or after round 5. Every remaining Fuzzy or Unknown becomes an open item. </limits> <output> During the interview: 1. Status: dimensions 1 to 7, each Clear, Fuzzy or Unknown. 2. Specifics to sort: | Detail | Proposed sort |, or NONE. 3. Questions. At the end: 1. Specifics: | Detail | BINDING / EXAMPLE / BACKGROUND / GUESS | Restated | 2. Decision Frame v1.0, dimensions 1 to 6: | Dimension | Answer | My words | 3. Open items: | ID (OPEN-n) | What is unresolved | What would close it | 4. Leaning, as its own block, so it can be left out of the brief. 5. Final line: "Reply Confirmed, or list the dimensions to correct." </output>

Build the brief

chat B (new)

Condition: you have a confirmed frame. Leave your leaning out.

<source_material> [Upload or paste the plan, proposal or work the decision rests on] </source_material> <decision_frame> [Upload or paste the confirmed Decision Frame, without your leaning] </decision_frame> <date>[Today's date]</date> <inputs> - source_material: the only source for claims, figures and assumptions. - decision_frame: sets the question, options, blast radius, criteria and open items; adds no facts. Keep its OPEN IDs. </inputs> <goal> Translate the source material into Decision Brief v1.0 that meets the standard, so I can decide with the evidence, assumptions and gaps in view. Success: every row passes DB1 to DB7. </goal> <standard> DB1 [Lint] Every claim, figure and assumption row has an ID and a quote or NOT IN SOURCE. DB2 [Review] Every claim the decision rests on is listed, with the evidence the source gives and where, or NO EVIDENCE. DB3 [Review] Every figure is recomputed from the source; a mismatch shows both values. DB4 [Review] Every assumption, stated or implied by the plan, is rated for impact and certainty with the anchors. DB5 [Lint] Every option in the frame has a cell for every criterion in the frame. DB6 [Review] Information that is absent and could change the decision is an open item, including anything the frame's blast radius or criteria need that the source does not cover. DB7 [Lint] The brief contains no recommendation and no ranking of options. </standard> <limits> - Labels: NO EVIDENCE (a claim with no supporting fact in the source); FIGURE FAILS (the recomputed value differs); CANNOT RECOMPUTE (the source lacks the inputs); NOT IN SOURCE (an assumption the plan relies on but never states); SOURCE TO OPEN (a document or person outside the source that would settle a row). - Impact: H = the decision's goal fails if this is wrong; M = cost or schedule slips; L = minor rework. - Certainty: H = the source gives evidence; M = stated without evidence; L = not stated or contradicted. - Option cells: what the source says about that option on that criterion, with row IDs, or NOT IN SOURCE. - Open items carried from the frame keep their IDs; new ones take the next unused number. </limits> <output> Title line: Decision Brief v1.0 1. Claims: | ID (CL-n) | Claim | Quote | Evidence | Where | Label | 2. Figures: | ID (FG-n) | Quote | Stated | Recomputed | Label | 3. Assumptions, high impact and low certainty first: | ID (AS-n) | Assumption | Quote or NOT IN SOURCE | Impact | Certainty | 4. Options: | Option | one column per criterion | Row IDs it depends on | 5. Open items: | ID (OPEN-n) | Missing information | How it could change the decision | 6. Sources to open: | Source | Rows it would settle | 7. Change log: | Version | Date | Row ID | Rule | Change |, with one line for v1.0. </output>

chat C (new)

Condition: the brief is built. Never in the chat that built it.

<source> [Upload or paste what the brief was built from] </source> <decision_frame> [Upload or paste the confirmed Decision Frame, without your leaning] </decision_frame> <artifact> [Paste the Decision Brief] </artifact> <standard> DB1 [Lint] Every claim, figure and assumption row has an ID and a quote or NOT IN SOURCE. DB2 [Review] Every claim the decision rests on is listed, with the evidence the source gives and where, or NO EVIDENCE. DB3 [Review] Every figure is recomputed from the source; a mismatch shows both values. DB4 [Review] Every assumption, stated or implied by the plan, is rated for impact and certainty with the anchors. DB5 [Lint] Every option in the frame has a cell for every criterion in the frame. DB6 [Review] Information that is absent and could change the decision is an open item, including anything the frame's blast radius or criteria need that the source does not cover. DB7 [Lint] The brief contains no recommendation and no ranking of options. Anchors. Impact: H = the decision's goal fails if this is wrong; M = cost or schedule slips; L = minor rework. Certainty: H = the source gives evidence; M = stated without evidence; L = not stated or contradicted. </standard> <gate> Impact (I): 1 Cosmetic: wording only; meaning unchanged. 2 Local: one row is weaker than it should be; nothing depends on it. 3 Misleading: a figure, rating or status is unclear, and someone could act on it. 4 Wrong: a figure, quote or rating differs from the source, or an assumption is shown as a fact. 5 Missing or invented: something the decision depends on has no row, or a row has no source. Ambiguity (A): 0 Two readers would read the row the same way. 1 Two readers would read it the same way with small differences. 2 Two readers could read it differently, or it can't be checked. Patch when I is 4 or 5, or when I is 3 and A is 2. Keep in every other case. </gate> <inputs> - source: the ground truth for facts and figures. - decision_frame: the ground truth for options, criteria and blast radius. - artifact: the brief under review. - standard: rules marked Lint are mechanical; rules marked Review need judgment. </inputs> <goal> Find every place the brief fails its standard against the source and the frame. Success: each failure has evidence and a corrected or added row, and every Review finding carries a gate result. </goal> <limits> - Check Lint rules row by row; every Lint failure is patched without a score. - Rate each Review finding with the gate; only Patch findings go in the patch block. - Evidence is the quoted source or frame line, or MISSING plus what is missing. - Keep existing IDs; number new rows from the next unused ID. - List failures only. When nothing fails, output only: NO FAILURES. </limits> <output> 1. Lint failures: | Row ID | Rule | Evidence | 2. Review findings: | Row ID | Rule | Evidence | I | A | Gate | 3. Patch block: | Row ID | Rule | Corrected or added row, in full | </output>

chat D (new)

Condition: the brief is fixed.

<decision_brief> [Paste the fixed Decision Brief] </decision_brief> <leaning>[The option you favor and why, in your own words]</leaning> <horizon>[When the outcome will be known]</horizon> <inputs> - decision_brief: the only source of facts. - leaning: the position to test. Its reasons are claims to test, not facts. - horizon: the time window for the pre-mortem. </inputs> <goal> The strongest case against my leaning, built only from the brief, so I decide knowing its weakest points. Success: every point cites brief row IDs, and the result names the evidence that would change the decision. </goal> <limits> - Cite brief row IDs for every point; label a point with no ID OUTSIDE BRIEF. - When a reason in my leaning has no row in the brief, list it as UNTESTED. - Case against: 200 words or fewer. </limits> <output> 1. Meeting notes

one saved prompt

Condition: after any meeting where something was decided or assigned. Record only in Teams, and say so before you start. Read the notes against what you heard, then send them under your name. Keep the transcript.

<transcript> [Upload or paste the meeting transcript] </transcript> <meeting> <purpose>[What the meeting was for, one line, or NOT STATED]</purpose> <date>[Meeting date, with the weekday]</date> </meeting> <inputs> - transcript: the only source for what was said; record only what it contains. - meeting: background for headings, wording and dates only. </inputs> <goal> Meeting notes every attendee can act on without rereading the transcript. Success: every decision, action and unsettled point in the transcript has exactly one item, each with its quote. </goal> <limits> - Decisions: what was decided, who decided, and the condition or "None stated". - Actions: one owner, one due date and one deliverable each, starting with a verb. - Anything that waits on another approval, and anything raised but not settled, including brief concerns, is an open item. - Dates: write each as a calendar date worked out from the meeting date, followed by the words used. - Uncertainty labels: NOT STATED (never said; also add an open item), UNCLEAR (two readings; quote both), CONDITIONAL (waits on another approval or event). - Names, numbers and examples appear only where they are part of a decision or action. </limits> <output> 1. P

Prompt Grader

before you share a prompt

Condition: before a prompt goes to your team, or after you change one. New chat. Rerun the revised prompt on the same input before you save it.

<rubric> Score each criterion 1 to 3. A criterion with nothing to act on scores 3 if the prompt states that case and how to handle it. | Criterion | 1 — Legacy | 2 — Developing | 3 — Standard | | --- | --- | --- | --- | | 1. Context | Vague, or includes material the task doesn't use. | Right material, but not delimited, or long material follows the goal with no restatement. | Only material the task uses, each piece labeled and delimited. Long material first; goal and output after. | | 2. Goal | Scripts the model's reasoning ("think step by step"). | Goal mixed with procedure the task doesn't need. | States the goal and success criteria. Steps only where their order is part of the result or a person takes part. | | 3. Limits | Vague bans ("don't be wordy") or no limits. | Basic limits; edge cases open. | Positive, specific limits. A named label or action for each kind of uncertainty. Claims cite sources when sources exist. | | 4. Output | Open, or described casually. | General form only ("use a table"). | Parts in order, each part's form and limits, any labels or IDs. Working before the final block. JSON only for programs. | | 5. Signal | Filler, personas, threats, tips. | Some padding. | Every sentence serves context, goal, limits or output. Definitions needed to stand alone stay. | | 6. Inputs and handoffs | Demands a specification from the person, or takes a raw chat or summary. | Treats every detail from a person as binding, or the next step can't check the output. | States how each input is treated. A person's free talk fills a stated rubric: named dimensions, each rated Clear, Fuzzy or Unknown against written definitions, with an exit rule; anything unresolved becomes an open item. Specifics are sorted as binding, example, background or guess; examples restated generally. The handoff translates what the person wants into a finite, testable artifact built to a named standard for its type (requirements: INCOSE rules, EARS syntax); it is a translation, never a summary; it lists open items and is confirmed by the person. A rubric encoding that standard checks it: a lint for mechanical rules, an anchored review for judgment. The next prompt takes only the artifact, in a new thread. | Rules: - Name no particular system, product, site, vendor or person; specifics arrive as inputs. - Remove unwanted details from the input instead of telling the model to ignore them. - A ban naming one exact item ("never write TBD") is allowed. - Show formats with empty templates, not filled examples. - State artifacts directly, without process references ("from Stage 4"). - Review in a new thread, with the output, the artifact it came from, and the person's words only. - Fix a thread's own artifact in that thread; hand on only the fixed artifact. - Follow the Agile cycle: build from the artifact, the person reviews the result, new or changed requirements go into the artifact as a versioned patch with a change-log line, and the next cycle starts a new thread from the revised artifact. - Before saving a changed prompt, rerun it on the same input. </rubric> <prompt_under_review> [PASTE THE PROMPT TO GRADE] </prompt_under_review> <purpose> [One line: what the prompt is for and who runs it. Write NOT STATED if unknown.] </purpose> <goal> Grade the prompt under review against every rubric criterion and rule, and write a revised prompt that does the same job and scores 3 on all six criteria. Success: every score below 3 has evidence and a fix, and the revised prompt changes only what the fixes require. </goal> <limits> - Use the rubric cell wording as the anchor for each score. - Evidence is the exact quoted line, or MISSING plus what is missing. - When the purpose is NOT STATED and a score depends on it, write ASSUMED and the purpose you assumed. - Keep the prompt's task, inputs and intended output. Record each change in the change list, outside the revised prompt. - Revised prompt length: the original length plus only what the fixes add. </limits> <output> 1. Scorecard: table with columns Criterion | Score (1-3) | Evidence | Fix; rows 1 to 6, then one row per rule broken. 2. Change list: numbered, one line per change, each naming its criterion or rule. 3. Revised prompt: one fenced code block, ready to paste. 4. Open items: decisions the person must make before saving the revised prompt, or NONE. 5. Retest: one line naming the input to rerun the revised prompt on before saving. </output>
