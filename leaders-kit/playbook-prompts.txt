SHARPEN A DECISION
Use it when you face a decision that lands on others.
Chats: A frame · B build (and fix) · C review · D case against · E announce
------------------------------------------------------------------------

1 · FRAME THE DECISION (chat A)
Condition: use when you face a decision that lands on others.
In class: skip this. decision_frame.txt and my_leaning.txt are its output.

COPY FROM HERE
<decision_talk>
[Talk freely: the decision, why now, the options you see, who it affects, and what you lean toward]
</decision_talk>
<inputs>
- decision_talk and my answers: free talk. Sort every specific I mention as BINDING, EXAMPLE, BACKGROUND or GUESS, and restate examples generally.
</inputs>
<goal>
Interview me, then translate my answers into a Decision Frame that a separate chat can analyze without talking to me. Success: every dimension is Clear or carried as an open item, and I confirm the frame.
</goal>
<dimensions>
1. Decision: the question, in one sentence.
2. Options: each option, including doing nothing.
3. Deadline: when it must be decided, and why.
4. Blast radius: who and what it lands on.
5. Reversibility: what undoing it would cost.
6. Criteria: what a good outcome looks like, and how it would be measured.
7. Leaning: which option I favor now, and why.
</dimensions>
<status_definitions>
Clear: an explicit answer with one reasonable reading.
Fuzzy: answered, but vague, conflicting, or open to two readings.
Unknown: not yet discussed, or I said I don't know.
</status_definitions>
<limits>
- Each round: the status of dimensions 1 to 7, then up to 3 questions, each with options A, B, C and D ("None of these, or I don't know").
- Exit: when all seven are Clear, or after round 5. Every remaining Fuzzy or Unknown becomes an open item.
</limits>
<output>
During the interview:
1. Status: dimensions 1 to 7, each Clear, Fuzzy or Unknown.
2. Questions.
At the end:
1. Specifics: | Detail | BINDING / EXAMPLE / BACKGROUND / GUESS | Restated |
2. Decision Frame, dimensions 1 to 6: | Dimension | Answer | My words |
3. Leaning, as its own block, so it can be left out of the brief.
4. Open items: | ID (OPEN-n) | What is unresolved |
5. Final line: "Reply Confirmed, or list the dimensions to correct."
</output>

2 · BUILD THE BRIEF (chat B, new)
Condition: use with a confirmed frame. Leave your leaning out.
In class: upload consolidation_plan.txt and decision_frame.txt, then paste.

COPY FROM HERE
<source_material>
[Upload or paste the plan, proposal or work the decision rests on]
</source_material>
<decision_frame>
[Upload or paste the confirmed Decision Frame, dimensions 1 to 6]
</decision_frame>
<inputs>
- source_material: the source for every claim, figure and assumption.
- decision_frame: sets the question, options and criteria; adds no facts.
</inputs>
<goal>
Translate the source material into a Decision Brief that meets the Decision Brief Standard, so I can decide with the evidence, assumptions and gaps in view. Success: every item passes DB1 to DB7.
</goal>
<standard>
DB1 [Lint] Every claim the decision rests on is listed, with its quote.
DB2 [Review] Every claim shows its evidence and where it is, or NO EVIDENCE.
DB3 [Review] Every figure is recomputed, and a mismatch shows both values.
DB4 [Review] Every assumption is tied to a quote or marked NOT IN SOURCE, and is rated for impact and certainty with the anchors.
DB5 [Lint] Every option in the frame is compared on every criterion in the frame.
DB6 [Review] Information that is absent and could change the decision is an open item.
DB7 [Lint] The brief contains no recommendation.
</standard>
<limits>
- Labels: NO EVIDENCE, FIGURE FAILS, NOT IN SOURCE, SOURCE TO OPEN.
- Impact: H = the decision's goal fails if this is wrong; M = cost or schedule slips; L = minor rework.
- Certainty: H = the source gives evidence; M = stated without evidence; L = not stated or contradicted.
</limits>
<output>
1. Claims: | ID (CL-n) | Claim | Quote | Evidence | Where | Label |
2. Figures: | ID (FG-n) | Quote | Stated | Recomputed |
3. Assumptions, high impact and low certainty first: | ID (AS-n) | Assumption | Quote or NOT IN SOURCE | Impact | Certainty |
4. Options: | Option | one column per criterion | AS IDs it depends on |
5. Open items: | ID (OPEN-n) | Missing information | How it could change the decision |
6. Sources to open: | Source | Why |
</output>

3 · REVIEW THE BRIEF (chat C, new)
Condition: use after the brief is built. Never in the chat that built it.
In class: upload consolidation_plan.txt, paste this, then paste your brief
inside <artifact>.

COPY FROM HERE
<source>
[Upload or paste what the brief was built from]
</source>
<artifact>
[Paste the Decision Brief]
</artifact>
<standard>
DB1 [Lint] Every claim the decision rests on is listed, with its quote.
DB2 [Review] Every claim shows its evidence and where it is, or NO EVIDENCE.
DB3 [Review] Every figure is recomputed, and a mismatch shows both values.
DB4 [Review] Every assumption is tied to a quote or marked NOT IN SOURCE, and is rated for impact and certainty with the anchors.
DB5 [Lint] Every option in the frame is compared on every criterion in the frame.
DB6 [Review] Information that is absent and could change the decision is an open item.
DB7 [Lint] The brief contains no recommendation.
</standard>
<gate>
Impact (I):
1 Cosmetic: wording only; meaning unchanged.
2 Local: one item is weaker than it should be; nothing depends on it.
3 Misleading: an owner, date, figure or status is unclear, and someone could act on it.
4 Wrong: an owner, date, figure or decision differs from the source, or a conditional item is shown as final.
5 Missing or invented: something in the source has no item, or an item has no source.
Ambiguity (A):
0 Two readers would read the item the same way.
1 Two readers would read it the same way with small differences.
2 Two readers could read it differently, or it can't be checked.
Patch when I is 4 or 5, or when I is 3 and A is 2. Keep in every other case.
</gate>
<inputs>
- source: the ground truth for what was written.
- artifact: the item under review.
- standard: the rules it must meet; rules marked Lint are mechanical, rules marked Review need judgment.
</inputs>
<goal>
Find every place the artifact fails its standard against the source. Success: each failure has evidence and a corrected row, and Review findings carry a gate result.
</goal>
<limits>
- Check Lint rules row by row and patch every Lint failure without scoring it.
- Rate each Review finding with the gate.
- Evidence is the quoted source line, or MISSING plus what is missing.
- Keep existing IDs; number new items from the next unused ID.
- List failures only. When nothing fails, output only: NO FAILURES.
</limits>
<output>
1. Lint failures: | ID | Rule | Evidence | Corrected row |
2. Review findings: | ID | Rule | Evidence | I | A | Gate |
3. Patch block: | ID | Corrected or added row |
</output>

4 · CASE AGAINST (chat D, new)
Condition: use after the brief is fixed.
In class: upload my_leaning.txt, paste this, then paste your brief inside
<decision_brief>. Horizon: the end of Q3.

COPY FROM HERE
<decision_brief>
[Paste the fixed Decision Brief]
</decision_brief>
<leaning>[The option you favor and why, in your own words]</leaning>
<horizon>[When the outcome will be known]</horizon>
<inputs>
- decision_brief: the only source of facts.
- leaning: the position to test.
- horizon: the time window for the pre-mortem.
</inputs>
<goal>
The strongest case against my leaning, built only from the brief, so I decide knowing its weakest points. Success: every point cites brief IDs, and the result names the evidence that would change the decision.
</goal>
<limits>
- Cite a brief ID for every point; label a point with no ID OUTSIDE BRIEF.
- Case against: 200 words or fewer.
</limits>
<output>
1. Case against: numbered points, each with its brief IDs.
2. Pre-mortem: three ways the leaning fails within the horizon, each with AS or OPEN IDs.
3. What would change the decision: | Evidence | Option it favors | How to get it |
</output>

CAPTURE IT · MEETING NOTES (one saved prompt)
Condition: use after any meeting where something was decided or assigned.
Record only in Teams, and say so before you start. Practise at home with
ops_review_transcript.txt.
------------------------------------------------------------------------

COPY FROM HERE
<transcript>
[Upload or paste the meeting transcript]
</transcript>
<meeting>
<purpose>[What the meeting was for, one line, or NOT STATED]</purpose>
<date>[Meeting date]</date>
</meeting>
<inputs>
- transcript: the only source for what was said; record only what it contains.
- meeting: background for headings and wording only.
</inputs>
<goal>
Meeting notes every attendee can act on without rereading the transcript. Success: every decision, action and unsettled point in the transcript has exactly one item, each with its quote.
</goal>
<limits>
- Decisions: what was decided, who decided, and the condition or "None stated".
- Actions: one owner, one due date and one deliverable each, starting with a verb.
- Anything that waits on another approval, and anything raised but not settled, including brief concerns, is an open item.
- Uncertainty labels: NOT STATED (never said; also add an open item), UNCLEAR (two readings; quote both), CONDITIONAL (waits on another approval or event).
- Names, numbers and examples appear only where they are part of a decision or action.
</limits>
<output>
1. Decisions: | ID (DEC-n) | Decision | Decided by | Condition | Quote |
2. Actions: | ID (ACT-n) | Action | Owner | Due | Deliverable | Quote |
3. Open items: | ID (OPEN-n) | Item | Raised by | Label | Quote |
4. Note to send, 250 words or fewer: decisions, then actions by owner, then what's still open.
</output>
COPY TO HERE
Read the notes against what you heard, then send them under your name.
Keep the transcript.

REQUIREMENT CARD (interview)
Condition: use when a goal needs a tool that doesn't exist yet. New chat.
------------------------------------------------------------------------

COPY FROM HERE
<goal_statement>
[The goal or problem, in your own words]
</goal_statement>
<inputs>
- goal_statement and my answers: free talk. Sort every specific I mention (system, product, number, name) as BINDING, EXAMPLE, BACKGROUND or GUESS, and restate examples generally.
</inputs>
<goal>
Interview me, then translate my answers into a Requirement Card a builder can design from without talking to me. Success: every dimension is Clear or carried as an open item, every requirement meets the card standard, and I confirm the card.
</goal>
<dimensions>
1. Job: what it must answer, draft or decide.
2. Users: who uses it, and when.
3. Sources: the documents and data it needs.
4. Rules: limits on data and users.
5. Sign-off: who is accountable for its output.
6. Measure: what gets faster or better, and how we would know.
</dimensions>
<status_definitions>
Clear: an explicit answer with one reasonable reading.
Fuzzy: answered, but vague, conflicting, or open to two readings.
Unknown: not yet discussed, or I said I don't know.
</status_definitions>
<limits>
- Each round: the status of dimensions 1 to 6, then up to 3 questions, each with options A, B, C and D ("None of these, or I don't know").
- Exit: when all six are Clear, or after round 5. Every remaining Fuzzy or Unknown becomes an open item.
- Card standard: each requirement is one EARS statement ("When [trigger], the [capability] shall [response]"), singular, verifiable and solution-free per INCOSE rules; it names a product only when that product is BINDING.
</limits>
<output>
During the interview:
1. Status: dimensions 1 to 6, each Clear, Fuzzy or Unknown.
2. Questions.
At the end:
1. Specifics: | Detail | BINDING / EXAMPLE / BACKGROUND / GUESS | Restated |
2. Requirements: | ID (REQ-n) | Requirement | Dimension | My words |
3. Open items: | ID (OPEN-n) | What is unresolved |
4. Final line: "Reply Confirmed, or list the IDs to correct."
</output>
