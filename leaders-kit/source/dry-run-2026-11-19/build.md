## 5. Open items

| ID | Missing information | How it could change the decision |
|---|---|---|
| OPEN-1 | Where the $1.2M savings figure comes from (carried from the frame). | If it is not supported or is much lower, the case for the move weakens or the facilities spend is not paid back. |
| OPEN-2 | The facilities spend amount, the one-time move and requalification costs, and the payback period. | The ask is to approve this spend. Without it the cost cannot be weighed against the savings. |
| OPEN-3 | Line B room and utilities: floor area, exhaust, gas and power capacity, and the Line A and Line B load figures behind "half capacity". | If Line B cannot take the tools or the combined load, the plan or its cost changes. |
| OPEN-4 | Evidence for Line B etch uptime and output under the combined load. | If uptime or output falls, customer delivery and the savings are affected. |
| OPEN-5 | Q3 audit scope, what it requires of moved tools, and the calendar dates of weeks 1-12 against the end of Q3. | If the audit would fail or the schedule slips past Q3, the plan misses its date or the audit. |
| OPEN-6 | Which customers run product on the moved tools, whether they need notice or requalification, and how supply is covered in the shutdown. | Customer surprises or missed deliveries could change the timing or conditions of approval. |
| OPEN-7 | Current etch support team size, workload on each line, and the expected combined load. | If the workload is not sustainable, staffing costs reduce the savings or risk uptime. |
| OPEN-8 | The schedule conflict: facilities work takes weeks 3-6 but the assumption says it fits in a two-week shutdown. Which line stops, when, and whether Line B runs during facilities work. | A longer or different shutdown changes output, schedule and cost. |
| OPEN-9 | Scope of "two etch tool sets" (the Goal says tools from Line A and Line B move; the timeline moves only Line A's), and what happens to Line A's space and costs after the move. | It changes the scale of the work and whether the savings can be realized. |
| OPEN-10 | The cost and time of reversing the move (the frame rates it high; the source gives no numbers). | If reversal is very costly, the risk of being wrong about the assumptions rises. |
