CHAPTER 7: ELECTROSTATIC CHUCK (ESC)

7.1 How backside helium works
Helium is fed through grooves in the chuck surface, under the clamped wafer, to carry heat from the wafer
into the chuck. The controller holds backside pressure at setpoint (typically 10 Torr) and reports the
flow it needs to do so. That flow is the He leak: helium escaping past the wafer edge and the chuck seal.
A normal chuck with a good wafer runs well under 1.0 sccm. The interlock stops the chamber at 4.0 sccm.

7.4 Removing and installing the chuck
  a. Vent, cool the chuck below 30 C, and lock out per site procedure.
  b. Lift the chuck assembly with the lifting fixture. Do not rest it on the seal face.
  c. Replace the outer chuck seal O-ring at every chuck removal.
  d. SEAT THE SEAL FULLY IN ITS GROOVE BEFORE LOWERING THE CHUCK. Run a finger around the full
     circumference. A seal that is rolled or partly out of the groove can hold at room temperature and
     open up as the chuck heats and cycles.
  e. Lower the chuck straight down. Torque the clamp screws in the star pattern in two passes.

7.6 He backside leak check (required after any chuck removal)
  a. Bring the chuck to process temperature (typically 60 C) and let it soak 30 minutes.
  b. Clamp a test wafer. Set backside pressure to the process setpoint.
  c. Record the He flow after 5 minutes.
  Acceptance: 1.0 sccm or less AT TEMPERATURE.
  NOTE: A check done at room temperature does not verify seal seating. A seal that is not seated
  typically reads above 1.0 sccm on the first runs at temperature and climbs as the chuck cycles.
